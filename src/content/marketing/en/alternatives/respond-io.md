## Define why you are evaluating alternatives
Write down the problem before booking demos: a required channel, a collaboration issue, a specific automation, a hosting requirement or a commercial model that no longer fits. Keep the requirements you already depend on alongside the new ones.

For example, a store may need better handover after a WhatsApp campaign, while a technical team may need control over hosting. Those are different buying decisions. There is no single alternative that is best for every situation.

## A shortlist organized by the job
| Candidate | A reason to investigate | What to verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ORQO | Shared conversations across WhatsApp, Instagram and Messenger, with customer context and AI-to-team handover | Required channel setup, operator workflow and plan allowances |
| Wati | WhatsApp-oriented campaign and support tools, with other documented channels | Exact channels, automation triggers and costs in the selected plan |
| Manychat | Social engagement and messaging automation | Trigger coverage, human follow-up, inbox seats and Active Contact usage |
| Chatwoot | Customer support with cloud and self-hosted options | Hosting responsibilities, channel availability and plan-specific automation |
| Intercom | Customer service with inbox, ticketing, help center and Fin AI | Seat and usage costs, required support workflows and channel coverage |

These are evaluation directions based on official product material, not rankings or a claim that one platform cannot serve another use case. You may also find that adjusting your current respond.io plan or process solves the original issue with less disruption.

## Compare billing units before comparing totals
A seat, an active contact, an AI outcome and an automation allowance are different units. Collect current quotes using the same baseline: team members who reply, connected accounts, typical monthly activity, AI workload and outbound messaging.

Repeat the estimate for a busy month. Separate subscription charges, usage, channel fees, implementation effort and ongoing administration. Check regional offers and billing commitments directly rather than copying a headline price into a budget.

## Plan migration before disconnecting anything
Inventory connected accounts, templates, customer data, consent records, workflows and integrations. Ask each provider which items can be transferred, which must be rebuilt and what history will remain accessible. Do not assume that buying a new inbox moves conversation history or WhatsApp numbers automatically.

Keep a rollback plan. Pilot a limited workflow, verify who handles replies and confirm the channel cutover process with the providers. Preserve required records in a secure, supported format before retiring old access.

## Use a scorecard your operators can complete
Ask the same teammate to handle a routine question, an exception, an AI handover and a returning customer in every shortlisted tool. Record whether the task succeeds without assistance and what setup it needed. Use the result with the full quote to decide whether a switch is worthwhile.
