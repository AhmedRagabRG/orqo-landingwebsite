## Turn a contact list into useful context
A phone number alone does not tell a teammate whether a customer is asking for the first time or following up on an earlier conversation. ORQO brings customer profiles, tags and segments together with the inbox. The existing product presentation also includes notes and stages for organizing follow-up.

Agree on a small set of useful tags before importing or adding contacts. A tag such as “delivery enquiry” should have a clear meaning. Avoid collecting details simply because a field is available; keep records relevant to the work your team needs to perform.

## Make handovers easier to understand
Record what the customer needs and what the team has agreed to do. For example, a store teammate can note that a product question needs a stock check, while the inventory system remains the authority for availability. The next person can see the context and complete the follow-up.

Review inactive or duplicate records regularly. Test how your connected channels identify customers and what happens when a person uses a different account. Do not assume that every identity is automatically reconciled.

## Segment with a specific purpose
Use segments to organize a relevant audience rather than sending the same message to every contact. Having a profile or tag does not itself establish permission to send marketing. Check consent and WhatsApp messaging requirements before using customer information for a campaign.

## Know where your CRM responsibilities begin
ORQO's customer-management scope is tied to conversations and follow-up. If you need accounting, a specialized sales process or an existing external CRM to stay authoritative, bring those requirements to the team. The public developer documentation covers contacts and conversations; confirm endpoint scope and permissions before building a synchronization.
