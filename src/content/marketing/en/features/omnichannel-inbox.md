## Stop moving between disconnected inboxes
A customer asks about a product on Instagram, then sends a WhatsApp message to confirm the details. When those messages live in separate tools, teammates have to reconstruct the conversation before they can help. A shared inbox gives your team a common place to handle the supported channels.

ORQO brings WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook Messenger messages together with customer profiles. Review the conversation and the available contact details before replying. Confirm how identity matching works for your connected accounts during setup; a customer using different identities should not be assumed to merge automatically.

## Give each conversation a next step
Assign a conversation to the teammate responsible for answering it. Keep internal context with notes and use tags to organize follow-up. When an AI agent hands a conversation to the team, the person taking over can work from the conversation rather than asking the customer to start again.

A practical daily routine is to review unassigned conversations, assign an owner, check the customer's context and close the loop after the reply. Agree on ownership rules before adding more automation.

## Where a shared inbox helps
For an online store, the inbox can hold product questions and delivery enquiries while a teammate checks the order in the store system. For a clinic, reception can answer location and opening-hours questions and pass appointment enquiries to the appropriate colleague. These are communication workflows, not a claim of automatic order or scheduling integration.

## Check your channel setup before switching
Bring one real example from each channel to a demo. Test a customer reply, an assignment and a handover. Check the number of connected accounts and inbox agents included in your chosen plan. Keep your existing inbox available until your team has confirmed the new workflow.
