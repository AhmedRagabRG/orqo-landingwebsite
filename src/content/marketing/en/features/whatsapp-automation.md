## Find the repeated step before building a flow
Automation works best when the trigger and desired result are clear. An enquiry outside business hours needs an acknowledgement. A new conversation needs an owner. A follow-up needs a reason and an appropriate time. Write those rules down before turning them into a workflow.

ORQO's existing website describes no-code automation for out-of-hours replies, assignment and follow-up. Confirm the exact triggers and actions you need during setup rather than assuming that any external system event is available.

## Separate service replies from outbound messages
A response to an incoming question is a different workflow from a campaign sent to a customer list. WhatsApp applies messaging rules, including requirements around consent and templates. Your automation should respect those requirements and stop inappropriate follow-up when a customer opts out.

Use a simple test case: a customer asks about delivery after closing time. Acknowledge the enquiry, explain when the team will return and assign it for review. Do not promise a dispatch time unless the business can support it.

## Keep a person responsible for exceptions
Add a clear escalation path for complaints, uncertain information and requests the flow cannot handle. Agree on what happens when the same customer replies again. Repeated acknowledgements can be frustrating; test the sequence as a conversation rather than as isolated messages.

## Launch one flow and review it
Test normal requests, missing information, duplicate enquiries and a customer asking to stop messages. Review the resulting inbox work with the teammate who will own it. Check the number of automations included in your plan before expanding. Automated messaging should support a service process your team already understands.
