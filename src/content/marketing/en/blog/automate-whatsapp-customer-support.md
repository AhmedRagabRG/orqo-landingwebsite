## 1. Map the requests your team actually receives
Review a small sample of recent support conversations without exposing customer data to unnecessary tools. Group the requests by the work they require: explain a policy, check an order, handle a complaint or coordinate a service. Separate repeated questions from decisions that need a person or a source system.

A useful first candidate has a stable answer and a clear boundary. Opening hours are usually easier to automate than a promise about tomorrow's delivery. Write down the approved answer and the situations in which it should not be used.

## 2. Understand the channel before designing the flow
The WhatsApp Business Platform provides APIs for business messaging. It is different from simply installing the WhatsApp Business App. A platform or provider can supply the inbox and workflow around those APIs; the exact setup, account requirements and supported features need verification.

Read the current WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy. Customer permission, messaging context and template requirements affect how an automated workflow should operate. An automation tool does not remove those obligations. Confirm the requirements for your intended messages instead of copying a flow from another business.

## 3. Prepare knowledge that can be maintained
Put approved service details and policies into the supported knowledge sources for your chosen product. Assign a business owner to each topic. When the delivery policy changes, someone should know which answer needs an update.

Avoid giving an agent vague instructions such as “always make the customer happy” when it lacks authority to change an order. A better scope is to explain the published exchange policy and hand exceptions to the team. Keep current stock and payment information with the system responsible for them unless a connection has been demonstrated.

## 4. Design the handover before the happy path
Decide what should happen if the answer is missing, the request is ambiguous, the customer is dissatisfied or the customer asks for a person. The handover needs an owner and enough context to avoid starting the conversation again.

In ORQO, the verified product scope includes AI replies, customer context, summaries and team handover. Test how those pieces work together for your actual enquiry. A readable summary is helpful, but the receiving person should still check the source messages when the request involves an important promise.

## 5. Test a whole conversation
Use a test account and sample data. Try a straightforward question, a typo, a follow-up that changes the subject, an Arabic question, an English question and a request outside the knowledge source. Ask for a person halfway through. Check that the next teammate can see what happened.

If the workflow sends outbound messages, also test an opt-out and a recipient who should not receive the message. Review the sending setup with the provider before running a larger campaign.

## 6. Launch narrowly and review useful evidence
Start with the chosen topic and keep people available to review replies. Record unanswered questions, incorrect information, repeated messages and missed handovers. These observations are more useful than treating every automated response as a resolved case.
