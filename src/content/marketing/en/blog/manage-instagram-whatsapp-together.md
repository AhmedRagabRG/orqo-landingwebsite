## Identify where the conversation breaks
Ask your team to describe the last enquiry that was lost between channels. Was nobody responsible? Did a teammate miss a previous reply? Did the customer use a different account? Each failure needs a slightly different remedy.

A shared inbox can bring channels into the same workspace, but it does not automatically solve identity matching or team responsibilities. Before choosing software, list the accounts you need to connect and the people who answer them. Separate account ownership from the day-to-day work of replying.

## Check connection requirements with the provider
Account types, permissions and supported messaging actions depend on the channel and provider. Ask for the current connection steps for your actual Instagram and WhatsApp accounts. A logo on a product page is not enough to prove that every message type or automation is supported.

Use a safe test message on each connected channel. Check where the conversation appears, which account receives the reply and what happens when permissions change. Plan how the team will spot and respond to a disconnected account.

## Create one ownership rule everyone can follow
For each incoming conversation, choose an owner or a clear assignment process. Avoid a situation where everyone can see the message but nobody knows whether to reply. A short internal note should state the customer's request and the next action.

During a shift change, review open conversations and commitments. Assign work needing coverage and identify which customer replies are waiting on information from another system. This routine matters even if most first responses are automated.

## Keep customer context accurate
A person may use a phone number on WhatsApp and a different identity on Instagram. Test how the tool presents those records. Do not assume a cross-channel customer profile is automatically correct in every case.

Keep tags understandable and notes relevant. If the customer asks about an order, check the order in the store's system. A conversation profile is helpful context, but it is not evidence that stock, payment or fulfilment data is current.

## Test the AI-to-person transition
If you use AI, start with a defined set of approved answers. Have the test customer ask an unexpected follow-up, switch language and request a person. The next teammate should be able to understand what was already said and continue without making the customer repeat the whole request.

ORQO's verified website scope includes a shared inbox for WhatsApp, Instagram and Messenger, customer context, assignment and AI handover. A pilot should establish whether that workflow fits your team's actual accounts, languages and staffing.

## Review the complete workflow after launch
Check missed enquiries, duplicate replies, incorrect assignments and unclear handovers. Review the causes before adding new rules. If the process is difficult for the people who operate it, more automation can make that difficulty harder to see.
